# Grow the vine — proposed first version

Status: menu entry and planning page added. Review together before implementing gameplay.

## Purpose

A quiet plant-growing game to accompany a meeting. One gentle tap brings a small piece of beauty to the vine, then the scene settles so attention can return to the conversation.

## Core interaction

- Start with a small plant vine already visible.
- Click or tap the vine: every activation adds something to it.
- Proposed growth sequence: a new leaf, a bud, then a beautifully drawn flower. Repeat with variations so the vine gradually becomes a flowering plant.
- Keep the vine easy to tap, with a generous interactive area. Support mouse, touch, and Enter or Space when focused.
- Give each tap a subtle response: a slight sway and a short, soft unfolding of the new growth. Avoid flashes, bouncing, or large movements.
- Keep all existing growth visible. Nothing withers or disappears when the player stops tapping.
- Stay still between interactions. No automatic growth, urgency, scores, streaks, or penalties.
- Include a simple return-to-menu control.

## Flowers and visual direction

Beautiful flower drawings are the main reward. Proposed style: delicate botanical illustrations, curved green stems, softly shaded leaves, and detailed petals in a small, harmonious colour palette. Use a calm background with enough contrast to see the plant clearly.

For the first version, propose three flower designs with a few leaf and bud variations. Review a sample of the artwork together before building the full scene. Keep blooms attached naturally to the vine rather than floating over it.

“Gentle taps” initially means light visual feedback and an easy interaction. Sound and vibration are optional ideas to discuss, not part of the proposed first version.

## Proposed scope

- One vine, one screen, one interaction.
- Each tap adds one visible growth element; no dragging or precision clicking.
- As the vine fills the scene, extend the composition gently to make space for more growth. Avoid abrupt camera jumps or shrinking the flowers until their detail is lost.
- Progress lasts for the current visit in the first version.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences: show new growth without sway or unfolding animation.
- Use the project's existing plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript setup.

## Implementation after agreement

1. Agree on the illustration style and whether the leaf–bud–flower sequence feels right.
2. Create and review a small flower, leaf, and vine artwork sample.
3. Build the initial vine and make every tap add one growth element.
4. Add gentle feedback, continued growth, keyboard access, and reduced-motion support.
5. Add a menu card using the approved botanical artwork.
6. Check repeated taps, small screens, keyboard operation, reduced motion, and menu navigation.

## Review during a meeting

Does each tap feel gentle and satisfying? Are the flowers beautiful at the displayed size? Can the player glance, tap, and return to listening without feeling pulled into repeated tapping? Refine those basics before adding more mechanics.
